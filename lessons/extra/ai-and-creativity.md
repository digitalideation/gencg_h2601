# AI and Creativity

> What do we learn, choose and share when AI becomes part of creative work?

We have already worked with rules, chance and systems that surprise us. AI adds another way of producing material. It also gives us questions about our choices, what we learn and how our work connects to other people.

Read one section at a time. Think for a moment, then compare with a partner. A sketch, a few words or an example is enough. You can take notes in any language and choose whether to speak to the class. Different answers are welcome.

Use these questions alongside the [AI mode of your current activity](../../readme.md#ai-in-this-course).

## 1. Making and learning

**What do we discover by making something?**

An idea can change when we draw, run a sketch or test a rule. Something fails, a rhythm appears, or another person notices a possibility. Trying, observing and changing our approach can teach us something we could not plan in advance.

AI might help us get past a technical problem. It might also let us skip a step through which we could learn. Some difficulties help us develop; others prevent us from taking part. We need to look at the situation.

**Example:** Your drawing machine makes an unexpected loop. You change one rule, compare the versions and decide that the loop is worth keeping.

**Discuss:** Think of a difficult moment while making something. What did you learn, and would you have wanted a tool to remove that difficulty?

## 2. Agency

**Who shapes the choices?**

Agency means having influence over what happens. It includes choosing an option, suggesting another, changing the question or saying no.

When AI proposes a result, we can ask how it relates to our intention. We might keep it, change it or reject it. We might also discover a new direction. Can we explain what changed our mind?

**Example:** A model gives your image smoother forms. You like the result, but the rough texture mattered to your idea. You can keep the texture, explore the smoother version or look for another approach. The attractive result gives you a decision to make.

Agency also concerns decisions we share. This connects creativity to democracy: who can participate, whose ideas are heard and who can question the rules? Making work and having influence are different opportunities.

AI might help someone express an idea in an unfamiliar language. We can then ask who will hear it, who receives recognition and who sets the conditions for sharing it.

**Discuss:** Choose a creative tool you use. What can you decide yourself, and which decisions would you like a say in?

## 3. Collaboration

**How do we create meaning together?**

Collaboration means working together so that other contributions can change the work. We bring experiences, languages and references. Listening, explaining and disagreeing can help an idea develop.

A symbol is a word, image, sound or gesture that stands for an idea. Its meaning can depend on who encounters it and in what situation. Stories and images help us share experiences and imagine how we might live together. Creating is one way of taking part in that shared world.

**Example:** You see the movement in your sketch as playful. A classmate finds it tense. Their response leads you to reconsider the rhythm, or to understand why you want to keep it.

AI can suggest words, images or ways to describe an idea. A familiar phrase might help someone communicate, or lose something specific about their experience. We can examine both possibilities.

When we call AI a collaborator, what do we mean? We can consider its suggestions alongside the people who discuss the purpose, respond to each other and take responsibility for the work.

**Discuss:** When has another person's response changed your idea? What would you want from AI in a similar situation?

## 4. Value and trust

**Why might the process matter to us?**

Imagine enjoying a work, then learning that AI helped produce it. Does your response change? What would you want to know?

People may value the result, the maker's intention, the experience behind it or the effort involved. These can matter differently to each person. Enjoying a work, trusting its description and deciding who deserves credit are separate questions.

**Example:** A maker values the choices and edits behind an image. A viewer wants to know which parts came from AI. They may both appreciate the image and disagree about what needs explaining.

Missing information about a process can affect trust. It does not tell us that the work is poor. Clear accounts of contributions give people more information for their judgment.

**Discuss:** Take the maker's and the viewer's perspectives. What might each value, and where could they disagree?

## 5. Our own practice

**What does this mean for the work we make?**

Return to an existing experiment, with or without AI. If useful, add a few lines beside a version in your [process journal](./journal.md):

- I wanted to explore...
- A contribution or unexpected result changed my idea when...
- I kept, changed or rejected... because...

Choose one decision you can explain to a partner. You can leave a question open.
